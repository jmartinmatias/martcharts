# Chart guide

Generated by `npm run catalog` from `src/core/guide.ts` and the catalog specs. Do not edit by hand.

For each chart: the jobs it does well and badly, the table it needs, and which other charts the same data makes, with an assessment of each switch. The full entry per chart, with the example data in full as a table and as CSV, is in [catalog/guide/](../catalog/guide/) (one Markdown file per chart, ready to hand to another model); the same as JSON is [catalog/guide/all.json](../catalog/guide/all.json); the page is [catalog/guide.html](../catalog/guide.html).

In code: `dataContract(spec, rows)`, `switchChart(spec, to, rows)`, `switchOptions(spec, rows)` and `guideMarkdown(spec, rows)` work on any data. Over MCP: `fft_chart_guide` and `fft_switch_chart`.

## How switches are judged

Each switch redraws the example's data as another chart, filling the new chart's options from the columns the current one draws, then:

- **Fit**: `fits`; `stretch` (it draws, but something is off, such as too many rows or text where dates are expected); or `no` (a column it needs is missing, or the data does not mean what the chart's marks mean, such as open, high, low and close for a candlestick).
- **Verdict**, on the current chart's main job: `better` keeps it and adds another; `same` keeps it; `trade-off` gains a job but does the main one less clearly; `different` answers another question; `worse` loses ground with nothing gained. A gain only counts when the data suits the new chart.

## area

A line with a soft filled area down to zero, for a trend where the amount itself matters.

**Jobs**: trend over time (best), how each group or period splits (good), comparing amounts across categories (possible).

**Best for**: Volumes over time where the amount itself matters: cash, revenue, traffic; A forecast with its uncertainty band; How a stacked total splits over time (two to four series).

**Least useful for**: Several series that cross: the fills hide each other (use line); Categories with no order (use bar); Values that go below zero.

**One row is**: One row per point in time, in order. **Built**: One point per row at x (in date order) and y; the area under the line is filled down to zero. Several y fields stack or overlap; a forecast field continues the line dashed, and a band (low, high) shades a range.

**Needs**: `x` (date), `y` (number, one or more). Example: 18 rows of `month`, `cash`, `forecast`, `low`, `high`. [Full entry with the data](../catalog/guide/area.md).

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `line` | fits | better | Better for actual against target or plan and comparing amounts across categories, and still good for trend over time. Cleaner with several series and when the trend matters more than the amount. |
| `horizon` | fits | same | Keeps the job (trend over time) with a different look. |
| `beeswarm` | fits | different | Answers a different question: better for how values spread, but gives up trend over time. |

## bar

Vertical bars comparing amounts across categories.

**Jobs**: comparing amounts across categories (best), ranking, top to bottom (good), trend over time (good), how each group or period splits (good), share of one whole (possible), actual against target or plan (possible), exact values and lookup (possible).

**Best for**: An amount across a handful of categories: revenue by region, orders by month; Two or three series side by side or stacked: Core and Plus by region; Monthly totals against a budget line.

**Least useful for**: Long category names (use hbar); More than about 15 categories (use hbar or treemap); A long continuous trend (use line).

**One row is**: One row per category, each named once. **Built**: One bar per row at x, as tall as y. Several y fields make grouped bars, or stacked ones with stacked (percent for shares); a lines field draws a line such as a target across the bars.

**Needs**: `x` (category), `y` (number, one or more). Example: 5 rows of `region`, `Core`, `Plus`, `total`. [Full entry with the data](../catalog/guide/bar.md).

## boxplot

A box plot per group on one shared axis: a box over the middle half of the values (first to third quartile), a line at the median, whiskers to the furthest values within 1.5 box-widths, and dots for outliers beyond.

**Jobs**: how values spread (best), comparing amounts across categories (good).

**Best for**: Comparing medians and spread across groups: deal size by region, handle time by team; Spotting outliers in analytic reviews.

**Least useful for**: Audiences who do not read quartiles (use beeswarm or ridgeline); Fewer than about five values per group; Totals.

**One row is**: One row per thing measured (a deal, a ticket, a response); the chart counts or spreads them. **Built**: Rows are grouped by group; each group's values give a box from the first to the third quartile, a line at the median, whiskers and outlier dots.

**Needs**: `value` (number). Example: 24 rows of `value`, `region`, `deal`. [Full entry with the data](../catalog/guide/boxplot.md).

## donut

Part-to-whole at a glance: a ring of up to 6 slices with the total in the middle and a legend of values and shares.

**Jobs**: share of one whole (best), a headline number (possible), comparing amounts across categories (possible).

**Best for**: Share of one whole with two to five parts: revenue by segment, budget by team; An executive summary where the total matters too.

**Least useful for**: More than six parts or close values (use hbar); Comparing several wholes (use bar stacked to percent); Change over time (use area).

**One row is**: One row per category, each named once. **Built**: One slice per row, its angle its share of the sum of value, with the total in the middle.

**Needs**: `label` (category), `value` (number). Example: 3 rows of `segment`, `revenue`. [Full entry with the data](../catalog/guide/donut.md).

## dumbbell

Before and after per item on one shared axis: a muted dot for before, an accent dot for after, joined by a line.

**Jobs**: change between two points (best), comparing amounts across categories (good), ranking, top to bottom (possible).

## variance

**Least useful for**: Data with no plan or budget (use hbar).

## waterfall

A bridge: how a total moves to another through increases and decreases, each floating from the running total, with totals standing on zero.

**Jobs**: what drives or moves a total (best), change between two points (good).

**Best for**: Bridges: what took revenue from Q2 to Q3, a profit and loss walk; Explaining a move from a start to an end.

**Least useful for**: Amounts that are not changes to one total (use bar).

**One row is**: One row per category, each named once. **Built**: Rows in order: totals stand on zero, and every other row is a change floating from the running total, up or down.

**Needs**: `label` (category), `value` (number). Example: 6 rows of `step`, `amount`. [Full entry with the data](../catalog/guide/waterfall.md).
